01 What you've purchased

Your Pose Reference Pack download is a ZIP file containing pose reference images prepared specifically for Sony cameras. Every image includes the visual pose, a posing prompt for your subject, framing guidance, and a short note to push the moment further.

The pack is designed to scroll through on your camera's LCD between shots, so you spend less time on your phone and more time present with the people in front of you.

Files prepared for one brand do not work on a different brand. If you are not sure your specific body is supported, write before you set up; we'll confirm.

02 Prep the SD card

To make sure the pose references display correctly, your SD card needs the right folder structure. The fastest way to set it up:

  1. Insert an SD card into your camera (slot 1 if you shoot dual-card).
  2. Format the card in the camera: MENU → Setup → Media → Format; confirm.
  3. Take one photo. This creates the DCIM/100MSDCF/ folder that Sony expects.
  4. Power off the camera and remove the SD card.
Heads up Formatting erases anything that's already on the SD card. If you're using a card with photos on it, back them up first; or, if the DCIM/100MSDCF/ folder already exists from prior use, you can skip the format and the test photo entirely.
03 About the test photo

The test photo from step 3 lands on the SD card with whatever filename your camera's File Number setting decides. On a fresh body that may be DSC00001.JPG; on a camera that's been shooting for a while, it might be DSC02473.JPG or anything in between. Either is fine.

The pose references are numbered starting at DSC00001.JPG, so most of the time your test photo and the pack don't collide and they sit happily side by side in the folder. If they do share a filename, your computer will ask whether to replace; you click Replace and the test photo gets cleanly overwritten by the matching pose.

04 Loading the poses

Step 1   Extract the download

  1. Locate the Pose Reference Pack ZIP file you downloaded.
  2. Mac: double-click the .zip file. Windows: right-click and choose "Extract All."
  3. Open the extracted folder. Inside you'll find DCIM/100MSDCF/ with the pose files.

Step 2   Insert the SD card into your computer

Use a card reader connected to your computer rather than connecting your camera directly over USB. Some camera bodies lock the file system in ways that can cause display issues with copied files.

Step 3   Find the right folder on the SD card

  1. Open the SD card on your computer.
  2. Open the DCIM folder.
  3. Inside, you'll see 100MSDCF (the standard Sony folder). The pose references go inside this folder; not loose in DCIM, and not in any other folder on the SD card.

Step 4   Copy the pose files

From the extracted ZIP folder, copy every DSC0xxxx.JPG file into the 100MSDCF folder on the SD card. If a file with the same name already exists (from your test photo), confirm Replace; that is a clean overwrite, not a deletion.

Important Copy only the .JPG files. Do not copy the ZIP itself, this PDF, or any other folders. Sony cameras can be confused by unexpected files or folder structures inside DCIM.

Step 5   Eject the SD card cleanly

Mac: click the ⏏ icon next to the SD card in Finder, or press cmd+E. Windows: use "Safely Remove Hardware." Don't yank the card before ejecting; Sony is sensitive to incomplete FAT writes.

05 Using the pack on your camera
  1. Insert the SD card into your camera.
  2. Power on the camera.
  3. Press the Playback button (▶).
  4. Scroll through the images with the joystick or scroll wheel.
  5. The pose references appear alongside any photos already on the SD card.
  6. Press DISP to reveal the prompt text and framing notes. Zoom in if you want to read details up close.
In practice: many photographers leave their camera in playback between shots, scrolling through references as they direct the couple. The pose reference stays on screen until you press the shutter or exit playback.
If the pack doesn't show Most Sony bodies want a small nudge to register newly-added files. Go to MENU → Setup → Media → Recover Image Database; confirm Yes; wait 10-30 seconds. The pack appears right after the rebuild.
06 Customizing your pack

The pack works best when you carry only the references you'll actually use. For a wedding day, you might pull 30-40 across getting-ready, ceremony, and portraits. For a portrait session, 15-20 from the relevant subset.

  1. On your computer, browse the extracted ZIP folder.
  2. Identify the pose references relevant to your upcoming session.
  3. Copy only those .JPG files into the SD card's 100MSDCF folder.
  4. For the next session, swap them out: copy in new ones, leave the rest on your computer.
Tip: keep the master ZIP unzipped on your computer. Treat the SD card as a working space that gets reorganized per session.

07 Bonus: embed your contact in every photo

Your Sony camera can automatically embed your name, copyright, and contact info into the EXIF metadata of every photo it takes. This protects your work and creates a path back to you if your gear ever gets lost. Set it once and forget it; it stays on across power cycles.

How to set copyright information on Sony

  1. Press the MENU button on your camera.
  2. Go to Setup → Setup Option → Copyright Info (exact path varies by body).
  3. Toggle "Write Copyright Info" to ON.
  4. Choose "Set Photographer" and enter your name.
  5. Choose "Set Copyright" and enter your business name, phone, email, or URL.
  6. Press MENU again to exit and save.
Every photo you take from that point on carries your contact info in its EXIF. A finder can read it from any photo using free tools like ExifTool or any browser-based EXIF reader.
08 If something doesn't work

If the pose references don't display, display as corrupted images, or your camera shows an error:

  1. Confirm you copied only the .JPG files; not the ZIP, this PDF, or any folders.
  2. Confirm the JPGs are inside DCIM/100MSDCF/, not loose in DCIM and not in another folder.
  3. Run MENU → Setup → Media → Recover Image Database on the camera; confirm Yes; wait for the rebuild.
  4. If only a subset displays, take the SD card out and back in, then run Recover Image Database again. Sometimes the index needs a second pass.
  5. If nothing on this page resolves it, format the SD card in the camera (back up any existing photos first), take a fresh test photo, and re-copy the pose files.

If the issue persists, email us with your camera model, firmware version, and SD card brand. Your feedback helps us refine the product.
